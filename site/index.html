One long video in.
A tagged album out.

Full albums and concert recordings get posted as a single video, with the track listing living only in the chapter markers. sleeve reads those markers and gives you back an album.

Source

Needs yt-dlp and ffmpeg. macOS and Linux. MIT or Apache-2.0.

How it works

Four steps, one download

Every track is cut from a single fetch of the source audio, so each one is one encode away from the original rather than two.

One generation

Tracks are cut from a single download of the source audio, never from an intermediate file.

Cutting from a transcode would stack a second round of loss on every track.

No inherited chapters

Each track drops the source's chapter list instead of carrying it along.

Without that, track 3 of an 8-track album advertises all 8 markers and players show it as the whole album — while its duration still looks right.

Padded, not cropped

Cover art is padded out to a square, after any bars already in the thumbnail are removed.

Padding costs background. Cropping costs the picture — and on a title card, it costs the text.

Cover art

The thumbnail is rarely the picture

Uploaders routinely export a square or 4:3 graphic into a 16:9 frame, so the real image is a sub-rectangle of the file. sleeve finds it, crops to it, and only then pads out to a square.

What lands on disk

An album, not a pile of clips

Every tag filled in

Title, artist, album artist, album, date, genre, track n/total, disc and comment — so an album stays one album instead of fragmenting.

Art in every file

Square cover embedded track by track, taken from the video's own thumbnail.

Named to sort

03 - Slow Ascent.m4a, zero-padded to the width of the track count, filed under Artist - Album.

Into Apple Music

One flag hands the finished tracks to your library — through the watched import folder, so no Automation permission is involved.

Look before you download

--list prints the track listing with no download at all. --dry-run shows exactly what would be written.

Tested without the network

93 tests, none of which spawn ffmpeg or reach the internet. The suite finishes in under a second, so it runs on every push.

Formats

Three, and one of them never re-encodes

YouTube's best audio is usually Opus. Asking for Opus out copies that stream straight through, so the result is bit-identical to the source — at the cost of embedded art, which the Ogg container will not carry.

Install

Two commands to first album

first 
brew install yt-dlp ffmpeg

sleeve drives both. It checks for them at startup rather than failing after a long download.

then 
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alexnodeland/sleeve/main/install.sh | sh

Detects your platform, checks the published SHA-256 before installing, and falls back to ~/.local/bin when /usr/local/bin is not writable. It never quietly reaches for sudo.

run 
sleeve https://youtu.be/VIDEO_ID

Artist, album and year come from the video title and upload date. Every one of them can be overridden.

Prefer cargo?

cargo install --git https://github.com/alexnodeland/sleeve

Pin a version

SLEEVE_VERSION=v0.1.1 SLEEVE_PREFIX=~/.local

A note on what you rip

sleeve makes your own copies playable and organized. Whether a given video is yours to download is between you, the uploader, and the rights holder.

Titles and artwork come from the uploader's own chapter list and thumbnail, so a rip is a personal-library artifact — not a released album, and it will not match anything in a store.
